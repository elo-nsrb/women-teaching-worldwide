Questions & answers

Everything you might want to ask before joining

If your question isn't here, ask it on the join form and we'll add the answer to this page.

Who it's for

Who can join?

Any woman who teaches mathematics, computer science, machine learning, statistics or a closely related subject at university level — anywhere in the world, at any stage of her career. PhD candidates who teach are included.

Do I have to be a professor?

No. PhD candidates, teaching assistants, lecturers, adjuncts, associate and full professors are all welcome. The woman two steps ahead of you is usually the most useful one to ask, and you are that woman for someone else.

Does my university have to be well known?

No, and this matters to us. There is no institution list, no ranking threshold and no invitation needed from anyone senior. We pay particular attention to women who have fewer opportunities for international collaboration.

My English isn't very good. Is that a problem?

No. English is the common language because it's the one most of us share, not because anyone is judging it. Write however you write. Nobody here is grading your grammar.

Is it only for women?

Yes. There are plenty of mixed spaces to discuss teaching; this one exists so that the questions women don't always feel free to ask elsewhere can be asked here.

What actually happens

What does it cost?

Nothing. There is no membership fee, no sponsor and no product behind this. If that ever has to change, we'd ask the community first.

How much time does it take?

As much or as little as you want. Reading without posting is completely fine and most people will do exactly that for the first months. Nobody tracks participation.

Where does the community live?

We start with a single WhatsApp group, because it's the one place nobody has to install anything new. If the group wants a forum, a mailing list or a shared library later, we'll build it — the join form asks what you'd actually use.

Will other members see my phone number?

Yes. In a WhatsApp group every member can see the other members' numbers. That is how WhatsApp works and we can't change it.

If you'd rather not share yours, tick "Keep me on email" on the join form. You'll get everything by email instead and you won't be added to the group.

What should I post?

Teaching questions, exercises, assessments, rubrics, slides, and the things you tried that didn't work. A half-formed question is welcome; you do not need to have thought it through first.

What doesn't belong: recruitment, promotion of products, and anything about a named student or colleague that you wouldn't say in front of them.

Can I reuse material someone shares?

Ask the author first, and credit her. Most people will say yes immediately — that's the point of sharing it — but it stays her work.

Joining and leaving

How do I join?

Fill in the form on the join page. We read every one and send the invitation by email. We don't post the group link publicly, which is what keeps the space what it is.

How long until I hear back?

We're two people doing this alongside full teaching loads, so it can take a few days. It always comes.

What happens to my data?

Only the organisers see what you send. We use it to contact you about the community and to decide how it should meet — nothing else, no sharing, no mailing tool. The legal basis is your consent, and we keep your details until you ask us to remove them. You can ask to see, correct or delete them at any time without giving a reason, and you can complain to your national data protection authority (in France, the CNIL).

How do I leave?

Leave the group, or write to us and we'll remove you and delete your details. No explanation needed, no follow-up email.

Can I help organise?

Please do. Two people cannot run a worldwide community, and we'd rather hand pieces of it over early than hold onto them. Say so on the join form.
