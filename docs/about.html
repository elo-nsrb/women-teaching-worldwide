How it started

Two teachers and a lunch table

It began at a teaching training programme in Paris, where two women — one teaching mathematics, one teaching machine learning — found they had the same questions and nobody to ask. So they started having lunch together once a week.

They talked through their classes, swapped exercises and exams, and worked out what to do about the lecture that hadn't landed. Sometimes one had an answer. Sometimes the useful thing was just hearing the problem described differently.

Teaching is treated as a private responsibility. You prepare alone, you fail alone, and you rarely see how anyone else does it. Two people comparing notes once a week was enough to change that for both of them.

Two people, however willing to help each other, cannot have all the answers.

So the question became: what if there were fifty of us? Two hundred? Teaching the same subjects in completely different systems, bringing approaches none of us would have thought of alone. That is what this is, and it only works if a lot of women sit down at it.

There is room for you.

Where this goes

We'd rather not decide it alone

We have no plan beyond the first hundred women. What the community becomes — a weekly question thread, a shared library, workshops, visits between institutions — depends on who joins and what they need.
